---
title: "Outside Home – Inside OSU"
author: Tasnin Nila
date: 2025-11-30 12:00:00 -0500
---
Pursuing higher studies abroad was a long-held dream of mine, much like many of my friends who achieved it before me. Whenever I scrolled through Facebook and saw their photos, I felt the joy of their gatherings and parties. Yet, behind those cheerful snapshots, I sensed something fragile—adjusting to new places, missing home-cooked meals, and the warmth of loved ones. I realized their journeys were not just about opportunity, but also about sacrifice. Then, with the grace of the Almighty, I received the chance to pursue a Ph.D. at Ohio State University (OSU). My heart bumped with joy—but beneath that joy was a quiet fear. Moving to a foreign land is challenging; moving with two young children felt like stepping into an unfamiliar world filled with uncertainty. I knew only one person in the entire OSU community—just one. Still, I believed that when the Almighty opens a door, He paves the way with unexpected kindness. And I was right.

I vividly remember our first night in Columbus. My friend and two of his friends—whom I had never met—welcomed us at the airport like family. After nearly two exhausting days of travel without Bangladeshi food, we finally enjoyed a warm Deshi meal at my friend’s home. It felt as though home itself had crossed the ocean to reach me. In that moment, I understood how powerfully community can hold you when you are far from everything familiar. Though Columbus was new to me, that night marked the beginning of a chapter filled with kindness and comfort.

Over time, I met more people who added warmth and color to my journey. The Bangladeshi community at OSU became part of my everyday life—faces that felt familiar, hands that offered help, hearts that shared the same longing for home. Our trips to different places brought laughter and adventure, but the most cherished moments were cooking together, sharing meals, telling stories, and watching our children play. In those moments, we were not just a group of families abroad—we were one big family, bound by shared roots and dreams. My kids are fortunate to have many Bangladeshi friends here. They run out to play with them, speaking Bangla with ease and joining BBQs, gatherings, and celebrations that give them the sense of belonging I once feared they might lose.

Every moment, friendship and shared meals have woven a safety net around us—one made not of blood, but of love. I feel profoundly blessed to be part of the OSU Bangladeshi community, which has given me a home thousands of miles from the one I left behind.
